This report covers earthquakes of magnitude 4.5 or larger recorded in the USGS catalog from 1990 to 2025, grouped into countries and regions. Annual counts swing by about 2.5×, partly because of catalog changes. Large earthquakes are rare (% are M6+) and activity is concentrated: regions account for half of all events. Counts and energy tell different stories — just 10 earthquakes release % of all seismic energy. Most earthquakes are shallow (%), yet magnitude barely changes with depth, and the USGS tsunami flag (available 2013–2025) rises from % of M4.5–4.9 events to % of M7+ events.

FINDING 08

Tsunami-flagged earthquakes are concentrated among larger earthquakes; depth matters much less.

Among earthquakes from –2025, the chance of a tsunami flag climbs steeply with magnitude. Most flagged events are shallow, but that is true of most earthquakes. The USGS tsunami field flags events that may have generated a tsunami — it is not a measured tsunami — and it is populated only from onward (every earlier event reads “not flagged”, even 2004 Sumatra and 2011 Tohoku), so this analysis uses 2013–2025 only.

METHOD & CAVEATS

How the data was built — and what to keep in mind

Everything on this site comes from one reproducible Python pipeline (scripts/) and a single USGS query.

  • Source: USGS ComCat earthquake catalog, FDSN web service (earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1), minmagnitude=4.5, 1 Jan 1990 – 31 Dec 2025 UTC, pulled . The catalog is revised over time, so numbers can drift slightly on a re-pull.
  • What one row is: one earthquake (event data). Each row has the time it happened (time_utc, analysed by year — 36 periods) and the country/region it belongs to (country_region, the group column — values), plus location, depth, magnitude, magnitude type, USGS significance and tsunami flag. There are rows and columns.
  • Rows dropped: events were downloaded; 125 were removed — 124 events that are not earthquakes (volcanic and nuclear explosions, mine collapses, rock bursts, landslides) and 1 event later revised below M4.5 — leaving . Nothing else is dropped or imputed; 62 slightly negative depths were set to 0.
  • Country / region group: the text after the last comma in USGS’s place field (78% of events), matched to Natural Earth country names and US states; places with no country (e.g. “south of the Fiji Islands”) use the named feature, and the few named events (e.g. “2010 Maule, Chile”) fall back to a Natural Earth polygon lookup. It is a mix of countries, territories (Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands are separate) and ocean features, not an official political boundary. The regions with 500+ events are listed below; the dashboard pools the rest as “All other regions”. Macro-region: Natural Earth continent for events on land or within 300 km of a coast, else the ocean basin (Russia east of 60°E counts as Asia).
  • Depth and magnitude classes: shallow < 70 km, intermediate 70–300 km, deep > 300 km; magnitude classes 4.5–4.9, 5.0–5.9, 6.0–6.9, 7.0+. About % of depths are USGS default values (10, 33, 35 km). events use mb magnitudes, the Mw family and others, so average magnitude mixes scales; it drifts from (1990s) to (2020s) as more small events are recorded.
  • Catalog changes: counts before about 2004 are lower partly because of changes in detection and reporting, so long-run trends in small-event counts should not be read as pure changes in Earth’s activity.

How every rate, ratio and average is computed

  • Counts and shares: a share is the number of events in a category ÷ the number of events in the whole set (224,699, unless the text says otherwise) × 100. “Per year” figures divide by the 36 years.
  • Year-to-year variation (CV): the sample standard deviation of the 36 annual counts ÷ their mean (finding 1; M6+ counts use the same formula).
  • Seismic energy: each event’s energy is E = 101.5·M + 4.8 joules (the standard magnitude–energy relation). A region’s energy share = the sum of E over its events ÷ the sum over all events; the “cumulative share” ranks events from largest to smallest E (findings 4 and 5). It is an estimate derived from magnitude, not a measurement.
  • Gutenberg–Richter b-value: b = log10(e) ÷ (mean magnitude − 4.45), the Aki maximum-likelihood estimate for 0.1-magnitude bins with a 4.5 cut-off. “Fewer by a factor of about 12 per magnitude unit” is 10b (finding 2).
  • Rank correlation: Spearman’s ρ between each region’s event count and its energy across the regions with 100+ events (finding 4); and between magnitude and depth across all events (finding 7). Rank = position when sorted from largest to smallest.
  • Depth: shares are events in a depth class ÷ events in the group shown; the median depth is the middle value of depth_km. In finding 7, the “share M6+” of a depth band = events with M ≥ 6 in that band ÷ all events in that band.
  • Tsunami flag rate: flagged events ÷ all events in the magnitude or depth class, using 2013–2025 events only (finding 8). The USGS flag means “may have generated a tsunami”, not a measured tsunami.
  • Dashboard: uses exactly the same definitions, calculated in the browser from the same data: the averages, maximum and totals are over the filtered events; “share of total energy” = energy of the events in view ÷ energy of all 224,699 events; the median uses the middle value of the filtered events; the Summary table lists the numbers behind the charts. Average significance is a USGS composite score that correlates with magnitude. Felt-report fields were left out because they are mostly missing.
