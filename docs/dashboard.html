Magnitude type

Magnitude can be calculated in different ways. Body-wave (mb) uses seismic waves that travel through the Earth and is the standard for small and mid-sized events; moment magnitude (Mw) reflects the size of the fault rupture and is preferred for large events; other types are older, local or regional methods.

Notes on reading this dashboard

  • Source: USGS ComCat earthquake catalog, M4.5+ earthquakes 1990–2025 (non-earthquake events removed), pulled . Times are UTC.
  • Magnitude types: body-wave magnitude (mb) and moment magnitude (Mw) are different ways of calculating magnitude, so averages across types mix scales. Body-wave (mb) covers about 78% of events and moment magnitude (Mw) about 20%.
  • Tsunami flag: USGS flags whether an earthquake may have generated a tsunami; it is not a measured tsunami. The field is only populated from 2013 onward — every earlier event reads “not flagged”, including 2004 Sumatra and 2011 Tohoku.
  • Depth: about 46% of events have a USGS default depth of exactly 10, 33 or 35 km (depth could not be resolved), so depth-class shares are approximate.
  • Country / region comes from USGS place text and Natural Earth names, not official borders; it mixes countries, territories and ocean features (e.g. Mid-Atlantic Ridge). Regions below 500 events are pooled as “All other regions”.
  • Energy is derived from magnitude (log₁₀E = 1.5M + 4.8, joules) and is dominated by a few huge events. Significance is a USGS composite score that correlates 0.88 with magnitude.
  • Averages and “average” rankings hide categories with fewer than 5 events. Event counts before ~2004 are lower partly because of catalog and reporting changes, not only real activity.
